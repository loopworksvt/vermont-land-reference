Legal checks before a harvest

practicelegal

A checklist of the laws and approvals the state's guidance says to review before cutting. Each line gives the rule, who it falls on and where it comes from. Confirm every line with the county forester before relying on it; two of the sources are 2012 and 2015.

Topic
legal
Who is involved
landowner, consulting forester, county forester, logger, agency
Related rules
R-VT-003, R-VT-004, T-VT-001, S-VT-003, S-VT-004, S-VT-006

When it fits

Before any timber harvest, including one by the owner, and again when the plan, the area or the buyer changes. For enrolled land the Profile's "Before You Act" report for cutting trees applies the vault's rules and tripwires to a particular parcel.

How it is done: the checklist

CheckWhat the source saysSource and date
Water quality (AMPs)Landowners and loggers must prevent discharges of sediment, petroleum and logging debris into streams and water bodies during and after harvest; failure is a violation with substantial penalty. See Water Quality and Logging Roads.Guidelines 2015; AMP booklet 2011
Heavy cutA landowner planning to heavily cut 40 or more acres in one treatment must file an "intent-to-cut notification" with FPR and receive approval. A heavy cut leaves residual stocking of acceptable growing stock below the "C-line". Harvests under 40 acres, or leaving stocking above the C-line, do not need it. If not already an approved treatment in a Current Use plan, an amendment is also needed. A search summary of FPR's page adds: file with the forestry district office at least 15 days before starting; an existing UVA plan can be an exemption; a $100 fee and penalties up to $50,000 are from older materials.Guidelines 2015 s.2.5; laws summary 2012; FPR page (search summary, not read in full)
Act 250 above 2,500 feetA land use permit is required for any logging operation over 2,500 feet elevation, issued by the District Environmental Commission.Guidelines 2015; laws summary 2012
WetlandsSilvicultural activities are allowed if the wetland's outlet configuration and the flow of water in and out are not altered and there is no draining, dredging, filling or grading; they must follow the AMPs and the deer-yard silvicultural standards; keep equipment maintenance and fueling on landings outside wetlands; keep landings in uplands or buffers (a landing needing no fill may be in a wetland only when the ground is frozen). Wetlands are Class One, Two or Three; vernal pools and buffers are protected.Wetland Rules; laws summary 2012; Guidelines 2015
Lake shorelandA shoreland law limits activities including trails and harvests near ponds and lakes; the silvicultural exemption requires an approved plan before harvest.Guidelines 2015
Stream alterationA Stream Alteration General Permit sets design requirements (span, height, embedment) for permanent bridges and culverts on perennial streams.Guidelines 2015
StormwaterA discharge permit may be needed for structures related to forestry.Guidelines 2015
Rare, threatened and endangered speciesTaking, possessing and transporting state-listed animals and plants is prohibited; a permit may be needed if a project could affect one.Guidelines 2015 (10 V.S.A. chapter 123)
Timber trespassCutting, felling or substantially damaging another person's trees without permission carries civil penalties per tree or treble damages; the landowner who authorizes a harvest must clearly and accurately mark the harvest unit, and a landowner whose poor marking results in unlawful cutting faces a civil penalty (the 2012 summary gives $250 to $1,000).Guidelines 2015; laws summary 2012 (13 V.S.A. ch. 13)
SlashRemove all slash for 50 feet from a public highway right-of-way or an adjoining woodlot boundary and 100 feet from standing buildings on adjoining property; keep main logging roads clear of slash for firefighting; the town forest fire warden can relieve the requirement if there is no fire hazard.Guidelines 2015; laws summary 2012 (10 V.S.A. ch. 83 s.2648)
Use Value Appraisal (enrolled land)Follow the forest management plan, report harvests, update the plan every 10 years, make sure AMPs are followed, and allow periodic conformance inspection by the county forester. Cutting contrary to the plan can mean land use change tax and removal from the program (T-VT-001 Cutting Contrary to the Plan).Guidelines 2015; UVA Minimum Management and Plan Standards 2026
Town ordinances and zoningTowns may not regulate silvicultural practices; they may restrict curb cuts, bridges and roads that affect harvesting and the siting of log handling areas or mills.Guidelines 2015
Easements, covenants, deed restrictions, certificationRead the deed and any easement or certification agreement; the timber sale contract must comply. A land trust or Forest Legacy easement may limit or direct harvests.Guidelines 2015
Chip harvesting for utilitiesOwners who supply chips to Vermont wood-fueled power plants need pre-harvest approval from Fish & Wildlife.Guidelines 2015; laws summary 2012
Trucking, worker safety, insurance, taxesMostly the logger's side: highway permits and limits, VOSHA and OSHA logging standards, workers' compensation. Timber income has tax rules; ask a tax professional.Guidelines 2015; laws summary 2012

Courtesies, not legal requirements (Guidelines): tell adjoining neighbors before harvesting; talk to utilities if lines are close; tell a recreational trail organization or the town if access will change; mark property and harvest boundaries.

Who does what

The landowner is ultimately responsible; the forester and logger usually carry the checks out; the county forester answers questions and approves plan amendments; the agencies named issue the permits.

Timing

Allow time: a plan amendment, the 15-day notice period for a heavy cut, any permit, and the contract. File harvest activity in the annual report due February 1 for enrolled land (S-VT-003 FMAR).

Costs and benefits

Costs are the filing fees (a heavy-cut fee is mentioned in older materials) and the time of the forester; the benefit is avoiding penalties, tax and loss of enrollment. See Harvest Costs Income and Incentives.

Risks and what to avoid

Starting before approval; an unmarked boundary; assuming a rule from memory.

Rules that apply

T-VT-001 Cutting Contrary to the Plan, S-VT-006 Activity Window, R-VT-003 Riparian ESTA Buffers, R-VT-004 Vernal Pool ESTA, UVA Minimum Management and Plan Standards 2026.

Not recorded here

The current text of any statute named; fees now in force; the Lake Shoreland Protection Act's thresholds; whether the Act 250 elevation limit has changed; rules for portable sawmills and chip harvesters beyond a mention.
