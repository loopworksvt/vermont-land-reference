Names

Red-belted conk, Fomitopsis pinicola. Family Fomitopsidaceae.

A hard, hoof-shaped bracket on conifer and hardwood trunks with a shiny orange-red band near its edge. A naming warning: the page says European and North American forms are now thought to be different species.

Range and habitat

Saprobic on conifer and hardwood deadwood, sometimes parasitic on living trees, causing a brown cubical rot; a perennial (MushroomExpert.com, Kuo, June 2022). The page says recent work found F. pinicola itself is strictly Eurasian and gives North American names: F. ochracea, F. schrenkii and F. mounceae.

How to identify

  • Semicircular or fan-shaped cap up to about 25 cm across, with a shiny, varnished orange-red to red zone and a thick whitish margin.
  • Older ones are hoof-shaped with crusty brown to black zones.
  • Underside whitish, with 3 to 4 pores per millimeter; no stem.
  • Flesh pale, leathery to woody; potassium hydroxide (KOH) turns the flesh red to reddish brown.

Look-alikes

  • Red varnished Ganoderma (the Ganoderma tsugae page of the same author, see Hemlock Varnish Shelf): their flesh is brownish and turns black with KOH.
  • Older Fomes fomentarius lacks red shades and has brown pores and flesh.

What the sources say about eating and poison

Not a food. MushroomExpert.com gives no edibility information.

Never eat a wild mushroom because of anything on this page. See Mushrooms in Vermont Woods and call a poison center (1-800-222-1222) if someone has eaten one.

What helps it thrive

It rots dead and dying wood, so dead standing trees and logs are its habitat. It can also grow on living trees.
