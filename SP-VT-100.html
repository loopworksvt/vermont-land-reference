Names

Woodchuck tick, also called groundhog tick, Ixodes cookei. A tick is an arachnid (related to spiders and mites), not an insect; this library files it with the insects and other small animals.

Range and habitat

Throughout Vermont (Health Department table). The table gives its habitat as the burrows of its host animals, rarely on vegetation.

How to identify (by season; look-alikes and the key difference)

The one photograph here (named for this species on its file page) shows a tick swollen with blood: a smooth gray body behind a small dark brown plate, with dark banded legs. A swollen tick is hard to name; the Agency of Agriculture accepts ticks for identification and the Health Department's page asks for reports through the Vermont Tick Tracker.

Signs on the land (tracks, scat, browse, nests, holes, calls)

Its habitat is the burrow of its host, so the sign is a woodchuck or other burrow.

Role in the forest (what it eats, what eats it, what it needs)

It feeds on blood. The table says its hosts live in burrows; it names no host animals.

What helps it thrive (not something to encourage; this is what it needs, so you know where it is)

Burrows of its host animals (Health Department).
