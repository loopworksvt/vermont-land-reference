Names

Fly agaric, Amanita muscaria. Family Amanitaceae.

The red-capped, white-spotted toadstool of picture books. The sources call it poisonous or psychoactive. A set of forms of it grows east of the Great Plains.

Range and habitat

Mycorrhizal, native to temperate and boreal forests of the Northern Hemisphere (Wikipedia). MushroomExpert.com's Amanita page says A. muscaria var. guessowii grows under hardwoods or conifers east of the Great Plains, var. persicina under conifers from the southeastern United States to New York, and var. flavivolvata is northeastern.

How to identify

  • The young fruiting body emerges from the soil looking like a white egg (Wikipedia).
  • The cap changes from globe-shaped to hemispherical to flat, with small white to yellow pyramid-shaped warts.
  • Gills white and free of the stem; spore print white; a ring and a cup-like volva on the stem (Wikipedia infobox).

Look-alikes

  • Relatives named by Wikipedia: A. pantherina, A. gemmata, A. farinosa, A. xanthocephala; A. breckonii has a buff cap and is tied to Pacific Northwest conifers.

What the sources say about eating and poison

Not a food. Wikipedia says eating it can cause poisoning, especially in children, from the compounds muscimol and ibotenic acid, that fatal poisonings are extremely rare, and that parboiling reduces toxicity. Its infobox says edibility is poisonous or psychoactive. Do not experiment.

Never eat a wild mushroom because of anything on this page. See Mushrooms in Vermont Woods and call a poison center (1-800-222-1222) if someone has eaten one.

What helps it thrive

Mycorrhizal with trees, so it needs living trees of the right kind; it fruits where those trees stand.
