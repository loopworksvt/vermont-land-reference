Vermont Family Forests Foundation checklist (updated 2020-03-06; © 2020 Vermont Family Forests): optimal conservation practices to protect water quality, site productivity, native biodiversity and carbon storage in actively managed forests. Applies most to northern hardwood and related natural communities. Landowners in the VFF-verified pool agree to follow it "to the maximum practical extent". It is a voluntary standard, stricter than Vermont's minimums. Key points:

Access (roads, skid trails, landings): follow USDA and Vermont Acceptable Management Practices standards; out-slope to 6%; average grade of access network 7% or less; access network no more than 5% of treated area; avoid slopes 35% or greater; log under frozen winter conditions, delay summer harvests until at least August 15, avoid spring harvest and rutting beyond the A horizon; only use when dry or frozen; landings on level stable ground away from protective strips; clean seed-free equipment; protect wetlands (muck/peat soils store about ten times the carbon of other regional soils) and special habitats (cliffs, caves, talus, beaver meadows, vernal pools, seeps, old growth remnants).

Buffers and crossings: protective strips per Table 4 (filtration width by slope); keep stream buffers at least 100 ft free of logging vehicles, little or no cutting; seed and mulch exposed soil with native species; bridges/culverts sized per Table 2 and set at right angles; remove temporary crossings promptly; restrict post-harvest access.

Vegetation management: single-tree and small group selection for northern hardwoods; keep group-selection openings under 0.25 acre; avoid clearcutting and whole-tree harvesting; forests store most carbon when left untouched, so avoid gaps not essential; soft edges; manage for at least 4 cavity/snag/decadent trees per acre (one over 21" DBH, four over 15"); at least 4 downed trees or logs 16+ ft per acre (one over 21", four over 15"); retain at least 3 vigorous, wind-firm legacy trees per acre over 19" DBH; favor native species; natural regeneration; avoid tree felling on slopes over 50%; leave biomass under 3" on site; remove invasives before harvest; organic-certified pesticides only, no agrochemical herbicides; biodegradable bar oil; no GMOs; limit residual stand damage to 10%; mark all trees before harvest; leave at least 30% of average annual growth on site; no domestic grazing; control deer; directionally fell low-value timber across slopes. Natural communities ranked S1/S2 (and S3-S5 with little past disturbance) need review by VT F&W Natural Heritage biologists.

Sensitive areas: wetland harvest/road work, or widening roads by more than 20%, requires permit/review by the Wetlands Office, Water Quality Division, (802) 241-3770. Protect cultural sites (terraces within 100 ft of permanent streams with south-to-west aspect may be prehistoric; consult Vermont Division of Historic Preservation).

Tables: (1) distance between drainage structures by road grade (e.g. 1% grade: 300 ft; 5%: 135 ft waterbars, 180 ft dips; 10%: 80 / 140 ft; 25%: 40 ft); (2) minimum culvert diameter by drainage area (4 ac: 15"; 16 ac: 24"; 40 ac: 36"; 80 ac: 48"; 160 ac: 66"); (3) seeding/mulching methods for roads, landings and crossings (e.g. hay/straw mulch at 60 bales/acre with annual ryegrass 40 lb/acre any time, winter rye 112 lb/acre, or soil conservation seed mix 42 lb/acre Apr 15-Jun 15 / Aug 1-Sep 15; native mixes only); (4) undisturbed forest buffer width by slope between road and stream (0%: 50 ft; 10%: 100; 20%: 140; 30%: 180; 40%: 220; 50%: 260).
