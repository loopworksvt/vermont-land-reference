Current Use basics: start here

Vermont's Current Use program (also called Use Value Appraisal, or UVA) taxes enrolled forest land on what it is worth as forest land, not on what it would sell for. In return you manage the land under a plan approved by the state. This page walks through what happens and when. Every step links to the full page.

Could my land qualify?

  • At least 25 acres enrolled, not counting the two acres around a house. See the acreage rule and the housesite rule.
  • At least 20 of those acres forested, on productive soil, and managed for forest products. Reserve Forestland is the exception for land kept as old forest.
  • A limited amount of open or idle land can be enrolled. See the open land limit.

Getting in

  1. Before you applyA plan and a map

    A licensed forester prepares the management plan and the property map for your land. Your county forester can refer you to a licensed consulting forester. Start months ahead, because the plan is the longest part. See the 2026 plan standards for what a plan must contain.

  2. September 1Application

    Send the application to the Department of Taxes. It takes effect the following tax year. See the application deadline.

  3. October 1Plan and map

    Send the signed plan and three paper copies of the property map to your county forester. See the plan and map deadline.

  4. The next April 1Plan takes effect

    The plan you filed is now the one you follow.

Every year

  1. All yearFollow the plan

    Do the work the plan prescribes, within three years before or after the year it names. Cutting outside the plan is a tripwire. See the three-year window and cutting contrary to the plan.

  2. February 1Report

    If you did any forestry work last year, file a report of it. No work, no form. See the yearly report.

Every ten years

  1. April 1 of the year the plan expiresUpdate the plan

    A plan lasts at most ten years. Start about eleven months ahead, because an update needs inventory data no more than five years old. See the ten-year update and what happens if a plan lapses.

If something changes

  1. Within 30 days of recording the deedOwnership changes

    The enrollment stays with the land. The new owner adopts the plan with a signature page, three copies of the plan map and a data entry form. See ownership changes.

  2. Any timeAddress changes

    Tell the Current Use office, with your parcel ID and owner ID. See address changes.

  3. Before you buildBuilding, a road, a subdivision or cutting off the plan

    The state calls these development, and they bring the land use change tax. See development on enrolled land.

  4. Before you split or withdraw landDropping under 25 acres

    If enrolled land falls below the minimum, the whole parcel can lose eligibility. See dropping below the minimum.

If you leave

  1. 30 days after the tax noticeLand use change tax

    Developed land is taxed at 10% of its full fair market value. The date of the notice isn't known until it arrives. See paying the tax and how the tax is figured.

Who to call

Start with your county forester. They walk your woods, talk through your goals, refer you to consulting foresters and handle Current Use for enrolled forest land. Questions about the application, the bill or an address change go to the Current Use office at the Department of Taxes.
