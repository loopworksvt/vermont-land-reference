Marking trees for harvest (best practices)

practicemarking

Marking tells the logger which trees to cut or leave and where the harvest begins and ends. Done well it prevents the commonest sale disputes: cutting unmarked trees, cutting past the line, and arguing afterward about what the paint meant. This note collects what the Vermont Agency of Natural Resources (ANR) says it does on state land and what Northern Woodlands says foresters do; both are good practice you can write into a private sale contract.

Topic
marking
Who is involved
landowner, consulting forester, county forester, logger

When it fits

Every timber sale, crop tree release or thinning where a crew will work from marks, and any harvest near a boundary. The state's own harvest-marking statute puts it plainly: 13 V.S.A. section 3603: 'As a best management practice, a landowner who authorizes timber harvesting or who in fact harvests timber should clearly and accurately mark the harvest unit with visible means.' It is a recommendation: the section sets out no penalty for failing to mark, but a mark is also what you point to if a neighbor's trees are cut.

How it is done

Decide what to mark: cut trees or leave trees. Northern Woodlands: if the minority of trees is to be cut, mark the cut trees; if the majority is to be cut, mark the leave trees. The ANR procedure has the same two methods:

Mark to Cut (the usual one)

  • Two opposing sides of each tree, plus a mark at ground level (a stump mark), in the same color throughout the unit. Blue is the state's preferred color; if blue is not on hand, any color is acceptable if used consistently across the whole project.
  • The product is tallied on the tree, 5 to 7 feet above the ground, with a code: a slash for sawlogs, a dot for pulp or pallet, a large 'C' front and back (no stump mark) for 'drop and leave' trees, two dots and a stump mark for 'logger's choice' (fell, girdle or harvest), 'G' for girdle only, 'L' for a leave tree (no stump mark), and an 'X' reserved for undoing a mistake during layout (as the procedure's table reads in the text extracted from it). The codes and the stump mark are what let the forester check the stumps afterward.

Mark to Leave (retention trees marked instead) is used for patch cuts, clearcuts and overstory removal, for seed-tree and low-density shelterwood cuts, and where a species (aspen, white birch or red maple, for example) is removed in large numbers but a few quality stems are kept. Leave trees get a single alternate-color horizontal stripe, front and back, with a stump mark; tally trees get a single blue spot on both sides and no stump mark. Where the two systems are mixed, keep the cut trees and the leave trees in different colors (blue for cut, orange or yellow for leave), and where a unit sits inside a larger one mark its boundary with double slashes in the leave color facing in on retained 'witness' trees (Specimen Trees covers marking trees to keep).

Mark the stem the way Northern Woodlands does: a ring of paint at breast height (4 to 6 feet) so the logger sees the tree from any direction, plus a mark on the base running below the cut (or leave) point so stumps can be checked after the harvest; if you know the direction the logger will approach, a single diagonal slash from breast height to the stump base works. (Kentucky's page, in a search summary not read, adds a spot at eye level as a theft deterrent.)

Mark the boundary (the 2023 procedure and Northern Woodlands):

  • The ANR procedure: all harvest boundaries should be clearly and accurately marked 'in a manner that will last throughout the duration of the planned harvest'.
  • Northern Woodlands: blaze the boundary line with a hatchet or brush axe before painting, then paint it red. ANR's own state-land property lines are painted high-visibility orange, with partial inholdings in red. Property-line colors differ between sources, so agree the color with the forester and surveyor.
  • The ANR blazing procedure for state land: blazed and painted trees as close to the line as possible, preferably within 3 feet and up to 6 feet where trees are few; a line blaze where the line passes through a tree, two quarter blazes for a tree up to 18 inches off the line, one side blaze for trees 1.5 to 6 feet away; new or refreshed blazes get three axe cross-hatches to tell them from natural or logging wounds; corners are witnessed with at least three sets of triple blazes with paint facing the corner monument, and use paint sparingly where the line is visible from neighbors' homes. Only a licensed surveyor sets a legal boundary; the sources read do not describe a landowner's own survey.

Say it in writing. The ANR procedure says how trees are designated must be clearly stated in the prospectus and contract and again at the showing and the pre-sale conference. Put the colors, the codes, the boundary paint and what to do with an unmarked tree into the contract (Selling Timber and Working With Professionals).

Choose paint and tools. Northern Woodlands names three tree-paint brands (Nelson, Trecoder and BarkMark), and describes paint guns that screw onto a quart can and backpack sprayers with tanks of 1 to 3 gallons, which are more efficient on larger jobs and keep paint away from the face on windy days; custom colors and chemical tracers to deter timber theft can be ordered. The ANR equipment list is a marking gun, paint or ink in the right colors, a Biltmore stick, loggers' tape, clinometer, compass, tally book or data recorder, flagging, GPS, a satellite messenger (SPOT) and phone, a small first-aid kit, insect and tick repellent and repellent clothing, the prescription and maps.

Use a marking guide. A marking guide used with the prescription designates trees by size, spacing, health and form (Northern Woodlands); the prescription sets the target basal area and number of trees per acre. The ANR minimum sawlog sizes (hardwoods 12 inches, spruce and fir 8, white pine 10, hemlock and red pine 10) are the minimum sizes for counting a tree as a sawlog in volume estimates on state sales (Harvest Methods and Equipment covers the work itself).

Timing

Mark before the showing to bidders and before the contract is signed, so they can see what they are buying (the procedure has a showing and a pre-sale conference). Paint does not last: Northern Woodlands says boundary paint lasts up to eight years while temporary tree-marking paint 'will begin to fade in just a few months'; a search summary of a Kentucky page says about three years for tree paint. Re-check marks before the logger arrives, and see Harvest Timing and Seasons for when the logging itself should happen.

Who does what

The forester usually marks, from the prescription and a marking guide; the ANR procedure assigns marking to forestry staff and seasonal employees under a stewardship forester. The landowner owns the boundary and agrees the marks; the logger follows them; the forester checks the stumps against the marks afterward (Harvest Close-out and Follow-up). On enrolled land the county forester approves the plan and can ask what was marked.

Costs and benefits

The sources read give no cost for marking. Benefit: fewer mistakes and disputes; a mark that matches a tally gives a count of trees to compare with the stumps; a clear boundary shows where a neighbor's land begins (a trespass cut by a logger is a different matter, see Legal Checks Before a Harvest).

Risks and what to avoid

  • Marks only on one side (a logger approaching from the other side sees nothing); no stump mark, so the forester cannot audit.
  • Mixed colors in one unit with no key; using the same color for cut trees and leave trees.
  • Fading paint on a sale that sits for months; heavy paint on trees along a road or near a neighbor's home.
  • Heavy equipment in mud season, which Northern Woodlands warns harms soil and the trees, and unclear communication between forester and logger.
  • Paint vapors: stand upwind of the paint while marking (ANR safety line). Wear permethrin-treated clothing and gaiters where ticks are common (Preventing Tick and Biting Fly Bites in the Woods).

Rules that apply

13 V.S.A. section 3603 (above): marking is a best management practice. The sources read give no rule on colors or codes for a private sale; use the contract. Where a harvest is next to state land, the state's own boundary is blazed and painted orange (Legal Checks Before a Harvest for the rules that do apply to a harvest).

Not recorded here
