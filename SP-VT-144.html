Names

Woodchuck, Marmota monax.

The woodchuck, also called groundhog, is a medium-sized burrowing rodent of fields, meadows and woodland edges, and a true hibernator.

Range and habitat

Fields, pastures, meadows, open woodland and forest edges, where loose, well-drained soil such as sandy loam lies near food. The Fish & Wildlife page calls it fairly common in Vermont and says its range has expanded over the past 200 years.

How to identify

  • Total length 42 to 69 cm, tail 9.5 to 19 cm; adults typically weigh 2 to 6.3 kg (Wikipedia).
  • Powerful short legs with broad, long claws; the tail is about a quarter of the body length; four white incisors.
  • A whistle is its alarm call; it also squeals, barks and grinds its teeth. It sits up and pulls plants with its front paws, and can climb trees to escape.

Signs on the land

  • Burrows with two to five entrances (one main entrance can have up to 11 openings), tunnels up to 45 feet long and a nest chamber lined with grass and dry leaves; abandoned burrows are used by other animals.
  • Dens in the open in summer and in more protected places in winter.

Through the year

Active by day, most after dawn and before sunset. It fattens in summer and fall, hibernates from October and emerges in March or April; its heart slows to about 15 beats a minute. Breeding begins in February or March; after about a month, two to six young are born in April or May, weaned at about six weeks.

Role on the land

Mainly plants: fruit, flowers, green vegetation (alfalfa, clover, grasses), and crops such as peas, corn, beans and oats; bark and twigs when food is scarce. Its tunneling aerates soil, and farmers and gardeners often consider it a nuisance.

What helps it thrive

Loose, well-drained soil, food nearby and cover for dens.
