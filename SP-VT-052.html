Names

Spotted Joe-Pye weed, Eutrochium maculatum, in the family Asteraceae.

Range and habitat

Go Botany answers "Yes" to native to North America (it gives no state-level native status), lists human-disturbed habitats, marshes, meadows and fields, shores of rivers or lakes, swamps and wetland margins, and records the plant as present in Vermont. It marks the plant as occurring only in wetlands (OBL). Minnesota Wildflowers lists sun; moist soil, along shores.

How to identify (by season; look-alikes and the key difference)

  • Flowers: disk flowers only, with no rays; each is blue to purple with five lobes, 6 to 50 per head, in a flat-topped cluster (Go Botany).
  • Leaves: in whorls, three or more at each node; simple, with toothed edges, 15 to 250 mm long, on stalks 5 to 20 mm long.
  • Stem: Go Botany says the common name refers to purple spots on the stems.
  • Look-alike (Go Botany): Eutrochium dubium, named without a comparison.

Role and management

The sources read give no mowing, grazing or control advice for this plant, and no statement about its value to wildlife.

What helps it thrive (light, soil and moisture, and how it is kept)

  • Wet ground. Go Botany (read in full) says it occurs only in wetlands (wetland indicator OBL) and lists its habitats as marshes, meadows and fields, shores of rivers or lakes, swamps and wetland margins, and human-disturbed places; it is a perennial with a rhizome.
  • Light and soil: from search summaries of nursery, seed and garden pages (not read): full sun to part sun, moist to wet soil, 4 to 7 feet tall, flowering about July to September, and attracting butterflies, including swallowtails. A nursery listing's call for rich, well-drained soil conflicts with the wetland habitat the other pages give; the wetland pages better describe where it grows wild.
  • Cutting: the sources read give no mowing response.

Legal status

Not on the Agency of Agriculture quarantine list as read on 2026-10-31 (effective 2026-06-16; the list's Watch List was not read). No other legal status was looked up. The quarantine list covers introduced plants.
