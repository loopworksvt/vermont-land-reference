Names

American Goldfinch, Carduelis tristis (Spinus tristis in newer lists).

Range and habitat

The Atlas says northern populations, including some in Vermont, are migratory (females go farther south than males), but some birds remain in Vermont all year; in the non-breeding season they travel in flocks to feed on birch catkins and grass and forb seeds. Audubon lists weedy fields, thistle patches, roadsides, open woods, edges and suburbs.

How to identify (by season; look-alikes and the key difference)

  • Size: 4 to 5 inches long (11 to 13 cm), wingspan 8 to 9 inches; about the size of a sparrow (Audubon).
  • Breeding male: bright yellow with black wings, tail and forehead. Breeding female: duller yellow-green. Winter adults: yellowish-brown to gray.
  • Other marks: bold white or buff wing bars on black wings, white tail spots and a notched tail.
  • Voice: a bright, bouncy "per-chick-o-ree", given in flight with each undulation.

Role and management

Audubon lists mostly seeds, especially from the daisy (composite) family, plus some insects in summer. The Atlas says experienced females may leave fledglings with the first mate and seek another male for a second brood. It gives no management recommendation; its timing note ties nesting to milkweed and thistle seed (Common Milkweed).

What helps it thrive (light, soil, shelter, food and the trees it uses)

  • Seeds of the daisy family, and late nesting. Audubon lists mostly seeds, especially composites such as thistles and goldenrods; the Atlas ties its nesting to milkweed and thistle seed (Common Milkweed, Canada Goldenrod).
  • What to do: the Atlas gives no management recommendation. An inference from what it eats, not a source's advice: uncut patches of seed-bearing wildflowers through summer would serve it, and they also serve bees (Pollinator Habitat on Forest and Field).

Legal status

Native birds are covered by the federal Migratory Bird Treaty Act, which prohibits taking (killing, capturing, selling, trading and transport are named) protected migratory birds without prior authorization (U.S. The Act applies to native species named in the treaties (50 CFR 10.13). Ask the county forester or the Fish and Wildlife Service before work near an active nest.
