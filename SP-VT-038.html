Names

Smooth brome (smooth bromegrass), Bromus inermis, a grass in the family Poaceae.

Range and habitat

Not native to Vermont. The fire-effects page calls it an exotic, cool-season grass native to Eurasia; Go Botany describes it as a Eurasian species introduced to North America. It grows in meadows, fields, roadsides and disturbed ground, only in non-wetland places (wetland status UPL), and is present in Vermont. The fire-effects page says it tolerates acid soils and all soil textures, prefers moist, well-drained soils, tolerates poorly drained ones, and invades after disturbance and persists. The UVM table says it is very tolerant of dry soils.

How to identify (by season; look-alikes and the key difference)

  • Growth habit (UVM): a tall, open sod-forming grass with extensive rhizomes. Leaves are rolled in the bud shoot.
  • Leaf: wide blades (5 to 15 mm, Go Botany) with a distinct crimp and an "M" marking on most leaves (UVM). Short, blunt (truncate) ligule, 0.5 to 3 mm; UVM says no auricles, Go Botany lists both.
  • Sheath: UVM says split; Go Botany says the edges are fused into a closed tube except possibly at the top; Oregon State says closed. Check a few plants rather than relying on this one feature.
  • Seedhead: a loose, open panicle 100 to 200 mm long with long spikelets 20 to 40 mm long (5 to 10 florets).
  • Height: 50 to 130 cm.
  • Look-alikes: the UVM table separates it from tall fescue (hairy short auricles, corduroy-grooved dark green leaves, bunchgrass) by its rhizomes, split sheath and the "M".

Role in the field

A hay and pasture grass. In the Cornell notes, smooth brome has large fluffy seed that is hard to sow in a mixture and will likely not persist in a mixture with alfalfa under intensive harvest. The fire-effects page calls it a common invader of disturbed prairie in the Great Plains.

Managing for it and living with it

The sources read give no mowing advice for smooth brome in a Vermont field; the fire-effects page mentions mowing only as a control method in grassland restoration.

Legal status

Not on the Agency of Agriculture quarantine list as read on 2026-10-30. No other legal status was looked up.
