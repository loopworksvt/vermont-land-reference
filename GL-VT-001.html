Management styles

  • Even-aged management: trees in a stand are about the same age; grown together, then mostly harvested at once (clearcut, shelterwood) and regrown. Managed on a "rotation".
  • Uneven-aged management: trees of many ages and sizes mixed together; a few trees cut every 15-20 years or so and gaps regrow naturally; the forest keeps a canopy.
  • Single-tree selection: an uneven-aged method that removes individual trees (poorly formed, diseased, crowded) and leaves the best. The gentlest harvest.
  • Group selection: an uneven-aged method that cuts small groups of trees together to make small openings for regrowth.
  • Shelterwood, clearcut, overstory removal: even-aged methods that remove most of the large trees in one or a few cuts.
  • Silviculture: the practice of growing and tending forests.
  • Rotation / cutting cycle: years between final harvests of even-aged stands / years between partial cuts in uneven-aged stands.
  • Intermediate thinning / TSI (timber stand improvement): cuts that improve a stand's quality or growth without regenerating it.
  • Crop tree release: cutting competing trees around the best trees (crop trees) so they grow faster.
  • Salvage cut: removing trees damaged or dying from storm, pests or disease.

Measuring a stand

  • Stand: a group of trees similar enough in species, age and condition to be managed as one unit.
  • DBH (diameter at breast height): trunk diameter at 4.5 feet.
  • Basal area (BA): the cross-sectional area of tree trunks at breast height, in square feet per acre; a measure of how crowded a stand is (tree volume, not a tree count).
    • Picture it: cut every tree in an acre off at 4.5 ft; each stump is a circle; add up the areas of the circles in square feet. That total is the basal area per acre.
    • General knowledge, not from the documents (unverified): fully stocked northern hardwood stands are often described as roughly 80-120 ft²/acre; basal area usually rises as trees grow, so the current figure is probably higher than 2017's; a selection cut typically removes poor-form trees first. A re-measurement at the 2028 plan update would give a current number.
    • How the prism works: looking through a wedge prism from a point, trees that appear "in" are counted; with a 10-factor each counted tree stands for 10 ft²/acre; the average over the points is the stand's basal area.
  • Residual basal area: basal area left after a cut.
  • Quadratic mean diameter (QMD): the diameter of the tree of average basal area.
  • Acceptable / unacceptable growing stock (AGS/UGS): trees of good form and vigor expected to grow into quality timber, versus poor-form or declining trees.
  • Prism / BAF (basal area factor): a wedge prism sighted from sample points; trees that appear "in" are counted and multiplied by the BAF (10 here).
  • Q factor: how quickly tree numbers drop from one diameter class to the next; a way to describe uneven-aged structure.
  • Site class / site index: a rating of how productive the land is for growing trees (Class I best, IV poorest).
  • Soil series: a named soil type.
  • Cover type: the dominant tree group (northern hardwood here: maple, beech, birch).
  • Regeneration: young trees coming up to replace the old.
  • Legacy tree / snag / cavity tree: old large trees left standing; dead standing trees; trees with holes used by wildlife.

Current Use (UVA) program

  • Current Use / UVA (Use Value Appraisal): Vermont's program taxing enrolled land at its use value, not market value, in exchange for following a plan (Current Use Program).
  • Enrolled vs excluded acres: the land in the program versus land left out (house site, lawn, outbuildings).
  • County forester: the state (FPR) forester who approves plans and amendments.
  • Management plan: the approved document describing what will be done in each stand and when; lasts 10 years (Forest Management Plan 2018-2028).
  • Plan amendment: a county-forester-approved change to the plan; needed before doing something the plan doesn't prescribe.
  • FMAR (Forest Management Activity Report): annual report (due Feb 1) of any forest work done the prior year (File Annual FMAR).
  • LUCT (Land Use Change Tax): tax due if enrolled land is developed or withdrawn; 10% of the changed land's assessed value divided by the CLA.
  • LV-314 (Request for Withdrawal): form to take land out of the program.
  • Contingent lien: the lien recorded on enrolled land to secure LUCT.
  • CLA (Common Level of Appraisal): a town's ratio of assessed to market value, used to compute use value.
  • SPAN (School Property Account Number): the state's ID for a parcel. OID (Owner ID): the owner's ID for eCuse.
  • Grand List: the town's assessed property record; eCuse: the online portal for Current Use filings.
  • Productive forest (Classes I-III): forest land of enough growing capacity to count as managed forestland;
  • ESTA (Ecologically Significant Treatment Area): a stand set aside for ecological protection (e.g. riparian area, rare community) with protective treatments instead of timber ones.
  • Reserve Forestland: a no-harvest category within Current Use.
  • Plan map: the map showing stands, enrolled and excluded land; must meet program mapping standards.

Current Use terms, acronyms and parameters

Compact reference. Sources: the 2026 Standards (UVA Minimum Management and Plan Standards 2026), the statute (32 VSA 3752 Definitions (Use Value Appraisal), 32 VSA 3757 Land Use Change Tax). Not legal advice.

Terms and acronyms

TermMeaning
Current Use / UVAVermont's Use Value Appraisal program: enrolled land is taxed on its use value (values by year: Use Value Rates) instead of market value.
PVRProperty Valuation and Review, a division of the Department of Taxes. Runs applications, the lien and the tax.
FPRDepartment of Forests, Parks and Recreation. Approves plans and inspects.
County foresterState forester who approves plans and amendments and inspects; does not write plans. Consulting forester: private licensed forester who writes them.
Plan / FMP10-year forest management plan with inventory, prescriptions and map.
Standards / MMSUVA Minimum Management and Plan Standards, effective May 31, 2026; the rulebook for new plans.
FMARForest Management Activity Report, due Feb 1 after any year with management activity; none if no activity.
LUCTLand use change tax: 10% of the fair market value of the developed (changed) land, as a separate parcel, divided by the CLA. Due 30 days after the notice.
FMV / CLAFair market value / common level of appraisal (the town's assessed-to-market ratio, set by PVR).
Use valuePer-acre tax value for enrolled land. Open/idle land uses the agricultural rate.
Contingent lienNotice recorded when land is enrolled; becomes a lien for the LUCT when development is recorded; runs with the land; cannot be moved or swapped.
DevelopmentBuilding a structure or road, mining or fill; subdividing to a parcel under 25 acres; cutting contrary to the plan during its term (32 V.S.A. 3752).
HousesiteThe 2 acres surrounding a dwelling. Not eligible land. Withdrawn with the LUCT; LV-314 is the Department's withdrawal form (ask the program office whether it is needed).
Managed forestlandEnrolled forest: at least 25 acres exclusive of the housesite, of which 20 are forested productive soils.
FPS / site classForested productive soils, site class I, II or III. Site IV = soils that can't grow 20 cu ft of wood per acre per year, often wetlands or shallow soils; enrollable with no timber.
ESTAEcologically Significant Treatment Area: land protected instead of logged (riparian areas, forested wetlands, natural communities, rare species, vernal pools, old forest). Stays enrolled.
Riparian areaESTA buffer: up to 100 ft around perennial streams, lakes and ponds; 50 ft along intermittent streams and wetlands. Lawn and maintained open land are excluded.
Vernal poolTemporary woodland pool in a natural basin, dry by late summer, no fish. State-significant ones get a 100 ft buffer as an ESTA; needs spring egg-mass photos and Fish & Wildlife review.
Reserve ForestlandNo-harvest category for old-forest values. Needs 50% (under 100 acres) of enrolled acres in significant and sensitive conditions; waives the 20-acre timber requirement.
Significant and sensitive conditionsESTAs, special places and sensitive sites, Site IV land, slopes of 35% or more. Slopes count only toward Reserve.
Open/idle ag landOpen land that won't be reforested, occasionally brush-hogged; not lawn or young forest. Limit: 25% of FPS (20% of the total). Taxed at the ag rate.
ConversionTurning enrolled forest into open land: needs FPR approval first; limit the lesser of 10% of FPS or 20 acres. Does not apply to land already open at enrollment.
AMPsAcceptable Management Practices for water quality on logging jobs; apply to all enrolled forest, stream crossings and wet ground.
Forms and IDsCU-301 enrollment application; LV-314 withdrawal form; eCuse online filing; SPAN parcel ID; owner ID.
AgenciesDEC: Environmental Conservation (wetlands 802-244-6951); DFW: Fish & Wildlife (reviews ESTAs); VSWI: Vermont Significant Wetlands Inventory; NRCS: USDA soil and conservation service.

Harvesting, selling and regeneration terms

Sourced from the Voluntary Harvesting Guidelines (2015), the FPR economics brochure, the AMP booklet (2011) and the UVA Standards 2026.

TermMeaning
StumpageThe value of timber as it stands, uncut; what a landowner is usually paid for.
Lump sum / mill scaleTwo ways to be paid for stumpage: an agreed price for all the trees harvested (lump sum), or a payment per tree cut, measured at the mill (mill scale).
MBF, board foot, cord, tonUnits of sale: a board foot is a 12 by 12 by 1 inch board and saw logs are sold by the thousand board feet (MBF); pulp and firewood by the cord (a 4 by 4 by 8 foot stack); pulp and chips often by the ton (2,000 pounds).
Whole-tree / cut-to-length / tree-lengthHarvest methods: the tree arrives at the landing whole; cut into product lengths at the stump; or delimbed and topped as a full-length stem.
Skid trail / truck road / landingA trail that brings logs to a landing; a road that takes logs from a landing; the cleared place where logs are gathered and loaded.
Bumper treeA tree left standing along a skid trail, especially at corners, to protect other trees from damage during skidding.
SlashThe residue left after cutting: branches, tops, bark, unused logs and broken stems.
AMPsAcceptable Management Practices: the required measures to keep sediment, fuel and slash out of water on logging jobs.
Heavy cutA harvest leaving stocking of acceptable growing stock below the C-line; one of 40 or more acres in one treatment needs an intent-to-cut notice to FPR.
Seedling / sapling / poletimberUnder the Standards: a seedling is under 4.5 feet tall; a sapling is taller than 4.5 feet and up to 4.9 inches DBH; poletimber is typically 5 to 10 inches DBH.
DBHDiameter at breast height, 4.5 feet above the ground.
Intent-to-cut notificationThe filing a landowner makes with FPR before a heavy cut.
Close-outThe work that stabilizes roads, trails, crossings and landings after a harvest; it should be done before the logger leaves.
County forester / consulting foresterA public employee who gives free advice and administers Current Use for the state, and does not write plans for private owners; a private professional paid to inventory, plan, mark, sell and supervise.
