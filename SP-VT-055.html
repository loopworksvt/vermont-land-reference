Names

Fireweed (narrow-leaved fireweed), Chamerion angustifolium (Go Botany, ssp. circumvagum), in the family Onagraceae.

Range and habitat

Go Botany answers "Yes" to native to North America (it gives no separate New England status), lists human-disturbed habitats, meadows and fields ("fields, roadsides, clearings") and records the plant as present in Vermont. Minnesota Wildflowers lists part shade or sun; moist soil, woodland edges and clearings, and shores.

How to identify (by season; look-alikes and the key difference)

  • Height: 30 to 200 cm.
  • Flowers: four petals 14 to 25 mm long, pink-purple (Go Botany also lists white), eight stamens and one style, radially symmetrical, on stalks 10 to 30 mm long arranged in a spiral around the flower spike.
  • Leaves: simple and alternate, 60 to 230 mm long and 7 to 34 mm wide, narrow to lance-shaped, with a pointed tip and veins that branch off the midvein at intervals.
  • Look-alikes (Go Botany): hairy willowherb (Epilobium hirsutum) has very hairy stems, while fireweed has few or no stem hairs. Purple loosestrife (Lythrum salicaria) has six petals and opposite or whorled leaves, while fireweed has four petals and alternate leaves and seeds with a tuft of white hairs. Purple loosestrife is on the Agency of Agriculture quarantine list (Tier 3), so the difference matters.

What helps it thrive (light, soil and moisture, and how it is kept)

  • Light: shade intolerant; it needs bare mineral soil in addition to high light to germinate (FEIS).
  • Soil and place: most commonly on disturbed ground, such as cut-over or burned forests and swamps; moisture is more stable and nutrients more available on a mineral soil seedbed.
  • Spread and disturbance: airborne seeds let it establish rapidly, then it forms large colonies by rhizomes; cutting stimulates the stem bases to make more shoots, and rhizomes sprout vigorously after surface fire.
  • Use to others: a preferred food of ungulates in some western states, a nectar source for hummingbirds, and used for nectar and pollen by butterflies.

Legal status
