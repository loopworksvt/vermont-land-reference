Water quality and logging roads (the AMPs)

practicewater

Vermont's Acceptable Management Practices (AMPs) for maintaining water quality on logging jobs are the state's required measures to keep mud, petroleum products and woody debris out of streams, ponds, lakes and rivers, and to keep trees along water to hold temperatures down. They apply to every logging job regardless of its purpose, including firewood cutting that is logging in scale.

R-VT-003, R-VT-004

When it fits

Every harvest, on every kind of land. Following the AMPs also keeps a logging job from needing an individual discharge permit.

How it is done

How the law works (2011 booklet, as of 2011).

  1. A violation happens only if there is a discharge; no discharge, no violation.
  2. If there is a discharge and the AMPs are properly in place, there is no violation.
  3. If there is a discharge and the AMPs were not followed, there is a violation.
  4. Slash (branches, bark or wood pieces) in a stream or other water body is automatically a violation, except temporary "brushing in" of small frozen streams in winter, with all slash removed.
  5. If the AMPs cannot be applied, a discharge permit can be sought from the Department of Environmental Conservation, but permits are likely only in extraordinary circumstances.

What the AMPs cover (the booklet's structure): during logging, truck roads, skid trails, surface water and stream crossings, protective (filter) strips along water, and log landings; after logging, the same list plus a summary chart for drainage devices. Examples from the 2011 text, which should be checked against the current version:

  • Plan roads and skid trails by walking the area; use old roads where well located; avoid ledges, swampy places and streambanks; flag, clear and grade before logging.
  • Drain road and trail surfaces with ditches, broad-based dips, turnups, waterbars or culverts, spaced closer on steeper grades (a spacing table is in the booklet); keep drainage structures clean; never let ditches empty straight into a stream: turn them out into a vegetated protective strip.
  • Skid trails go across a slope at a gradual angle rather than straight up it; use silt fence, hay bale checks or diversions to keep sediment from trails out of streams.
  • Truck road crossings of permanent streams are over a bridge or culvert; skid trails may ford only stable gravel or ledge beds; frozen-winter "brushing in" must be removed before spring runoff or when use ends, whichever comes first; keep logging activity out of stream channels except to build the crossing, and work in low water.
  • Seed and mulch exposed soil near streams soon after disturbance. (Distances and rates are in the booklet; do not rely on a figure quoted from memory.)
  • After logging: close out roads, trails, crossings and landings (below).

Support: FPR's Watershed Forestry program, five AMP Technical Advisory Teams (an FPR forester, sometimes with a DEC enforcement officer), temporary skidder and truck bridge rentals and cost-share for temporary bridges, crane mats and steel culverts (search summaries of FPR pages).

Who does what

The landowner is ultimately responsible for applying the AMPs; a good timber sale contract transfers the duty to the logger during the harvest; the landowner is responsible for maintaining erosion-control devices after logging is finished. The forester inspects during the job (Selling Timber and Working With Professionals). Report a suspected violation or ask for help from the FPR district forestry office or the AMP forester.

Timing

EXTREME CAUTION in the spring wet season and wet weather, when erosion potential is highest (Harvest Timing and Seasons). Do crossings in low water in as short a time as possible.

Costs and benefits

Costs: construction and maintenance of drainage structures, bridges and culverts; seeding and mulching; the time to inspect. Benefits: protection from liability (the AMPs legally protect the logger and the owner when followed), less equipment wear from muddy roads, soil kept on site, fish habitat protected (the booklet lists these). The 2011 booklet gives penalties for significant water pollution including removal of wastes and restoration at the violator's expense and fines up to $10,000 a day; check the current penalty. A permanent crossing left in place after logging, or one on a large stream, may need a Stream Alteration General Permit (see Legal Checks Before a Harvest).

Risks and what to avoid

Slash in a stream; ditches draining into water; fords on soft beds; undersized culverts that wash out in spring runoff; leaving erosion devices unmaintained after the logger leaves.

Rules that apply

The AMPs themselves; the Vermont Wetland Rules (silvicultural activities allowed only if the wetland's flow is not altered and no draining, dredging, filling or grading, with landings kept to uplands or buffers or placed on frozen ground); the riparian buffers an enrolled plan uses (R-VT-003 Riparian ESTA Buffers); vernal pools (R-VT-004 Vernal Pool ESTA).

Not recorded here

The current AMP numbers (spacing, culvert sizing, protective strip widths), the 2018 revisions, and the post-logging chart.
