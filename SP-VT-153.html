Names

Short-tailed weasel, Mustela erminea.

A tiny, fast, fierce hunter, the second smallest member of the weasel family, known in winter by its white coat as the ermine.

Range and habitat

Wooded areas to grasslands, needing heavy cover but avoiding dense forest; it prefers open woodland and forest-field edges near water.

How to identify

  • Males 19 to 33 cm long and females 17 to 27 cm, with tails of 7 to 12 cm; males average about 258 g and females under 180 g (Wikipedia).
  • Sandy brown above and white below in summer; in the north, a completely white winter coat except for the black tail tip.
  • Eyes round and black, ears short and rounded; the tail is over a third of the body length.

Look-alikes

  • Long-tailed weasel: a relative with a narrower habitat range.
  • Mink: larger.

Signs on the land

  • It does not dig its own burrows: nests in rock or wood piles, hollow trees, under buildings, or in the dens of its prey; a den may have several galleries within 30 cm of the surface.

Through the year

Breeds in late spring or early summer, with a delay before development resumes in March; young are born in April or May, in litters of 4 to 15 (average six or seven).

Role on the land

A predator of voles, shrews, cottontails, chipmunks and nesting birds; fruit and berries in summer. It also caches extra food and defends its territory.

What helps it thrive

Heavy cover, rock or wood piles, hollow trees, open woodland and forest-field edges near water.
