Names

Red fescue, Festuca rubra, a fine fescue in the family Poaceae.

Range and habitat

Mostly not native. Go Botany says the species is largely non-native in New England; a native subspecies (pruinosa) is limited to Atlantic coast beaches and near-coastal places, with only a historical Vermont record. It grows in disturbed or maintained ground, meadows and fields, cliffs and rocky slopes, and is present in Vermont. UVM says the fine fescues are drought and shade tolerant. The UVM 2004 chart lists red fescue among the perennial pasture grasses of Vermont.

How to identify (by season; look-alikes and the key difference)

  • Growth habit (UVM): one of the fine fescues: low-growing, narrow-leaved perennial grasses, drought and shade tolerant. Red fescue leaves tend to fold and roll and are narrow; sheep and hard fescue leaves are almost always rolled and are quite narrow. The seedhead is a narrow, contracted panicle.
  • Leaf: blades 0.3 to 3 mm wide, up to 15 cm long (Go Botany). Ligule very short (0.1 to 0.5 mm). No auricles. The sheath is a closed tube, reddish brown, shredding on older leaves.
  • Seedhead: an open panicle, 30 to 300 mm long, with 3 to 10 flowers per spikelet (Go Botany) (UVM calls it narrow and contracted).
  • Height: 15 to 130 cm.
  • Whether it forms a tuft or spreads by rhizomes depends on the subspecies; Go Botany says subspecies rubra has rhizomes and commutata does not.

Role in the field

UVM says the fine fescues are usually unpalatable for grazing livestock.

Managing for it and living with it

The sources read give no management advice for red fescue.
