Names

Black-throated Blue Warbler, Setophaga caerulescens.

Range and habitat

The Atlas profile says nesting success depends on a healthy shrub layer, and that heavy deer browsing and overly mature, open understories reduce cover. The species winters mainly on Caribbean islands (mostly the Greater Antilles) that have lost over half their forest. The Atlas gives a global population of about 2 million, stable or increasing trends, and says Partners in Flight ranks it "secure". Audubon lists the interior of hardwood and mixed forests, especially with dense understory.

How to identify (by season; look-alikes and the key difference)

  • Size: 4.3 to 5.1 inches long (11 to 13 cm), wingspan 7.5 to 8.7 inches; about the size of a sparrow (Audubon).
  • Male: can look mostly black in shadow, with a white belly and a white wing spot.
  • Female: plain dark above and buff below, with a white wing spot (faint on some young females) and a dark cheek patch.
  • Voice: a husky, rising "zwee-zwee-zwee".

Role and management

Audubon lists mainly insects (caterpillars, moths, crane flies) and spiders in summer. The Atlas says the song, long nesting season and use of the lower forest layers make it easy to detect, and its profile names deer browsing as a cause of lost cover (White-tailed Deer).

What helps it thrive (light, soil, shelter, food and the trees it uses)

Forest songbirds in general (the Guide, chapter 6). Keeping forest intact is possibly the most important thing: large patches not cut up by roads, buildings or big openings hold more forest birds and produce more young, and an opening changes the forest for more than 200 meters from its edge (more nest predators and cowbirds in small or fragmented patches of under 30 acres). Keep a variety of habitats (hardwood, softwood, wetland, floodplain) and stream and wetland buffers; keep some old forest (over 100 years) with cavity trees and dead wood, and where a mostly forested landscape has under 5 percent young forest consider 1- to 5-acre patches of young forest. Different species use different layers: ovenbirds nest on the leaf litter, black-throated blue warblers in a dense understory of hobblebush.

  • This species: it nests in thick understory such as hobblebush, and the Atlas names deer browsing as a cause of lost cover, so a crowded deer herd works against it (White-tailed Deer).

Legal status

Native birds are covered by the federal Migratory Bird Treaty Act, which prohibits taking (killing, capturing, selling, trading and transport are named) protected migratory birds without prior authorization (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service page, read through a summarizing fetch 2026-10-31). The Act applies to native species named in the treaties (50 CFR 10.13); that list was not checked for this species, and the page read says nothing about nests or about cutting trees in nesting season. Ask the county forester or the Fish and Wildlife Service before work near an active nest.
