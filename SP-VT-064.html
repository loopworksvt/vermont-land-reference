Names

Common St. Johnswort (common St. John's-wort), Hypericum perforatum, in the family Hypericaceae.

Range and habitat

Not native: Go Botany answers "No" to native to North America and records the plant as present in Vermont. It lists human-disturbed habitats, meadows and fields, and shores of rivers or lakes, and marks it as occurring only in non-wetlands (UPL). Minnesota Wildflowers gives origin as Europe and lists sun; fields, roadsides, woodland edges and disturbed soil.

How to identify (by season; look-alikes and the key difference)

  • Height: 40 to 80 cm.
  • Flowers: yellow, 15 to 30 mm across, petals 8 to 12 mm long and 5 to 6 mm wide. In Go Botany's comparison the petals are dotted with black only near the edges, and the sepals have abundant black dots and lines.
  • Leaves: simple and opposite with smooth edges, 20 to 40 mm long and 3 to 15 mm wide.
  • Look-alike (Go Botany): spotted St. Johnswort (Hypericum punctatum), which has black dots over more of the petals.

What helps it thrive (light, soil and moisture, and how it is kept)

  • Light: requires abundant light for best development (FEIS); in a greenhouse test, plants at about half of full daylight declined sharply.
  • Soil and water: prefers well-drained, coarse-textured soils and does not do well on poorly drained sites; develops best in pastures, open grasslands, abandoned fields and disturbed places.
  • Spread and disturbance: reproduces by seed and by sprouting from lateral roots and root crowns; vegetative spread was stimulated by grazing, fire and defoliation. The review gives no mowing response.
  • Use to others: one Michigan field was heavily visited by at least 35 bee species. The page that was read does not describe livestock poisoning.

Legal status

Not on the Agency of Agriculture quarantine list as read on 2026-10-31 (effective 2026-06-16. No other legal status was looked up.
