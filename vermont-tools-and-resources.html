The tools

Vermont ANR Natural Resources Atlas: View aerial imagery and natural resource layers, and draw and measure areas, for example to lay out a candidate exclusion area

Stability: The state's memo of 2026-01-06 says the Atlas runs on a retiring Esri technology; it is expected to keep operating through 2028, but browser updates could make it perform poorly or stop working before then; a replacement is planned.

ANR Natural Resources Atlas

What it is for

The Agency of Natural Resources' web map. In a 2026 inspection follow-up a county forester recommended it, and the state's Survey Library, to an owner for exploring an exclusion area on aerial imagery before a forester prepares the plan map. It is a viewing and sketching tool; it does not produce the plan's maps (the Standards point mapping requirements to Property Valuation and Review, see UVA Minimum Management and Plan Standards 2026 §5).

Open it at a place

Add a map position to the address: https://anrmaps.vermont.gov/websites/anra5/?center={x},{y}&scale={scale}.

  • {x} and {y} must be Web Mercator meters (EPSG:3857), not latitude and longitude. A latitude/longitude pair was not read as a position: the map opened far zoomed out.
  • {scale} is the map scale denominator. At 5000 the map showed roughly 500 meters across in a small browser window (more on a large screen). Use about 7500 for a parcel of 30 acres and 1000 to 1500 for a two-acre area; adjust to the window.
  • To convert longitude and latitude to Web Mercator meters: x = lon × 111319.4908, y = 6378137 × ln(tan(π/4 + lat × π/360)), with the angles in degrees.
  • Tested 2026-10-29: with center and scale in Web Mercator the map opened at the intended place on aerial imagery, at scales 5000 and 1500. level was not tested separately.
  • An owner's own links are generated into their Profile by the Map Links report, so no coordinates are kept in this note.

What it can do (seen)

The viewer is a Geocortex HTML5 viewer (the start page showed version 4.15.3, updated 2024-11-06). Its ribbon has these tabs:

  • Map Tools: Sign In/Out, Open, Save, Save as, Share, Export, Save to ArcGIS, View My Content, Create Account, Print.
  • Navigation, Identify, Query, Select, Measurement.
  • Map Data: Show Layers, Layer Catalog, Add Layers, Imagery by Year, Upload Data.
  • Draw: Point, Text, Line, Freehand, Freehand Shape, Circle, Polygon, Rectangle; Edit, Erase, Clear; Export Drawings; Drawing Area.

Drawing, tried in Chrome on 2026-10-29: choosing Polygon shows a yellow prompt ("Click or tap locations to define a polygon. Double-click/tap to finish"), adds Enable Snapping, Select Snapping Layers and Styles buttons, and makes the ribbon one row taller, which moves the map down. Click each corner, then double-click the last one. Esc cancels an unfinished shape. Once a shape exists, Export Drawings and Clear become available. Drawing Area then shows a pop-up alert reading "Drawing Area = 0.41 acres." (the form of the message; the figure was for one test polygon).

Export Drawings, tried on 2026-10-29 with one polygon: the Atlas asks "The '.shp' file is about to be downloaded. Confirm?" and, on OK, downloads Export.zip to the browser's download folder. It held an Esri shapefile bundle (Polygon.shp, .shx, .prj, .dbf, .cpg; 911 bytes in all). The coordinate system in the .prj is WGS 1984 Web Mercator Auxiliary Sphere (the same as {x} and {y} in the link format). The polygon came out as a closed ring of five points (the four corners, the first repeated) with the exact drawn corners. Converted to longitude and latitude, the geodesic area was 0.406 acres, matching the 0.41 acres Drawing Area reported. The attribute table was empty: no name, label or description is kept, so the owner has to say what each shape is.

With several shapes (two polygons, one line and one point drawn together, tried 2026-10-29), one Export.zip came out with one shapefile set per geometry type: Point., Polygon. and Polyline. (15 files, each set with its own .prj in Web Mercator and an empty attribute table). A set appears only for a type that was drawn (the earlier polygon-only export held only Polygon.). Inside a set the records follow the order the shapes were drawn (the two polygons came out first-drawn first), and a line keeps every point clicked. There was still a single "The '.shp' file is about to be downloaded. Confirm?" prompt.

The map opens on aerial imagery (attribution to VCGI and Sanborn Map Company) with red outlines that look like parcel lines; the Layers panel was not opened to confirm.

Not tested

  • Export Drawings with text labels, styles, circles, rectangles or freehand shapes. Only polygons, one line and one point were exported. Whether text labels and styles are kept, and how freehand and circle shapes come out, is unknown. Record order was seen only for two polygons.
  • How accurate Drawing Area is. One test polygon (about 52 m by 31 m on the map's scale) read 0.41 acres, about what its drawn size implies; no comparison with a surveyed area was made.
  • Save, Save as, Open, Layer Catalog, Add Layers and Upload Data: these were greyed out when checked without signing in. No account was created and nothing was signed in to.
  • The Share link format, and whether it keeps drawings.
  • Whether the red outlines are the parcel layer.
  • A phone-width window: in one (about 750 pixels wide) the map area stayed blank while the side panel showed. Use a laptop-sized window.

Stability

The state's memo "Natural Resources Atlas Modernization," dated 2026-01-06 (Agency of Digital Services and Agency of Natural Resources), says the Atlas depends on technology being retired and "will continue to operate through 2028"; "future browser updates could cause the Atlas to perform less effectively or cease to function during the interim period." It asks users to be aware the platform is in a transition period, to consider its limited lifespan in their planning, and to watch for notices on the Atlas home panel. A new Atlas is planned. Keep any boundary you draw in your own records, not only in the Atlas. Memo: https://anrmaps.vermont.gov/websites/ANRA5/documents/AtlasModernizationMemo.pdf

Recipes

Lay out a candidate area. Open the Atlas at the parcel (a generated link), open Tools, then the Draw tab. Choose Polygon, click each corner on the imagery, and double-click the last one. Click Drawing Area and read the acres in the alert. Use Measurement to check distances (not tried). Treat the result as a sketch: for the plan, a consulting forester prepares the maps.
