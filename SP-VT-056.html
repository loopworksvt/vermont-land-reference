Names

Harebell (Scotch bellflower), Campanula rotundifolia, in the family Campanulaceae.

Range and habitat

Go Botany answers "Yes" to native to North America, lists terrestrial habitats, cliffs, balds and ledges, meadows and fields, ridges and shores of rivers or lakes, and records the plant as present in Vermont. It marks the plant as usually in non-wetlands (FACU). Minnesota Wildflowers lists part shade or sun; rocky slopes, open woods and meadows.

How to identify (by season; look-alikes and the key difference)

  • Height: 10 to 80 cm.
  • Flowers: bell-shaped and hanging or bending down, blue-purple, with five petal lobes and five sepals; 10 to 30 mm across and 10 to 55 mm long.
  • Leaves: the basal leaves are broad and rounded to heart-shaped, while the stem leaves are narrow and linear, up to 80 mm long and 10 mm wide and alternate; lower leaves are larger and toothier and on longer stalks than upper ones.
  • Look-alikes (Go Botany): Campanula patula and Campanula persicifolia; the page names them without a comparison.

Role and management

The sources read give no mowing, grazing or control advice for this plant, and no statement about its value to wildlife.

What helps it thrive (light, soil and moisture, and how it is kept)

  • Light: full sun, partial shade or full shade (Lady Bird Johnson Wildflower Center).
  • Soil and water: dry, sandy, well-drained soils; the page says it is easily grown in a variety of sites, including rock gardens.
  • Use to others: attracts hummingbirds.
